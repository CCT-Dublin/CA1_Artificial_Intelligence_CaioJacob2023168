# Ethical, Legal and Social Impacts of Facial Recognition

## 1. Introduction

Facial recognition is an application of Computer Vision that can be used to compare facial images and identify possible matches. The technology can provide useful support in areas such as identity verification and criminal investigations. However, its use can also create ethical, legal and social risks, particularly when an incorrect result contributes to a decision affecting an individual's rights.

The case of Robert Williams and the Detroit Police Department provides a real-world example of these risks. Williams was wrongly arrested after facial recognition technology produced an incorrect result during a police investigation. The case demonstrates that the impact of an AI system depends not only on its technical performance but also on how people interpret and act on its results.

## 2. Ethical Impacts

### 2.1 Accuracy and Reliability

One of the main ethical concerns is whether facial recognition results are reliable enough to support decisions with serious consequences.

In the Robert Williams case, a poor-quality surveillance image was used in a facial recognition search and produced an incorrect result. The case demonstrates that a facial recognition result should not automatically be treated as proof of identity.

This creates an ethical responsibility for organisations using the technology. When an AI system produces a possible match, users need to understand that the result may contain errors and should be independently verified before consequential decisions are made.

### 2.2 Fairness and Demographic Differences

Fairness is another important ethical concern. Facial recognition systems can have different error rates depending on the algorithm, task, image quality and demographic characteristics.

NIST research has found demographic differences in the performance of tested facial recognition algorithms. These differences can involve both false positives and false negatives, although their size and direction vary between algorithms and applications (Grother, Ngan and Hanaoka, 2019).

The Robert Williams case should not be presented as proof that every facial recognition system is equally biased. Instead, it demonstrates why organisations should independently test the systems they use and consider how errors may affect different groups.

### 2.3 Human Oversight

The case also raises questions about the role of human decision-making.

An AI system did not physically make the decision to arrest Williams. Instead, its result became part of a wider human investigation. This distinction is important because responsibility for the use of an AI system remains with the organisation and the people operating it.

Human oversight should therefore involve more than simply having an employee look at an AI-generated result. Investigators should understand the limitations of the system, question uncertain results and obtain independent evidence before taking serious action.

### 2.4 Accountability

Organisations using facial recognition need clear rules defining who is responsible when the system produces an incorrect result.

The Williams case demonstrates the importance of accountability because the consequences of an incorrect identification were experienced by a real person rather than by the technology itself.

Clear procedures, staff training, documentation and independent review can help organisations identify errors and determine whether the technology was used appropriately.

## 3. Legal Impacts

### 3.1 Wrongful Arrest and Civil Rights

The most direct legal impact in the Williams case was the wrongful arrest of an individual based partly on an incorrect facial recognition result.

Williams was detained for approximately 30 hours before being released. The incident subsequently became the subject of legal action against the Detroit Police Department.

The case demonstrates that the use of AI in law enforcement can have consequences involving fundamental rights and individual freedom. It also shows that organisations can face legal and institutional consequences when AI-supported investigations result in serious errors.

### 3.2 The 2024 Settlement

In June 2024, a settlement introduced new restrictions and safeguards concerning the Detroit Police Department's use of facial recognition technology (University of Michigan Law School, 2024).

The measures included restrictions on arrests based solely on facial recognition results and requirements for independent and reliable evidence. The settlement also included training requirements and an audit of relevant cases dating back to 2017.

This is important because it demonstrates how a real-world incident can lead to changes in organisational policy and oversight.

### 3.3 Legal Context in Europe

The Williams case took place in the United States, so its legal outcome should not be directly treated as European law.

However, facial recognition also has significant legal implications in the European Union. Under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), biometric data used for uniquely identifying a person receives special protection under Article 9 (European Parliament and Council of the European Union, 2016).

The EU Artificial Intelligence Act also establishes specific rules concerning biometric identification and certain uses of remote biometric identification (European Parliament and Council of the European Union, 2024).

These regulations demonstrate that the use of facial recognition involves not only technical considerations but also legal requirements concerning privacy, biometric data and individual rights.

## 4. Social Impacts

### 4.1 Impact on Individuals and Families

The social consequences of an incorrect facial recognition result can be significant.

Robert Williams was arrested at his home in front of his family and was detained for approximately 30 hours. The incident therefore affected more than the accuracy of an AI system; it affected an individual's freedom, dignity and family life.

This illustrates how an apparently technical error can become a serious social problem when the technology is used in situations involving people's rights.

### 4.2 Public Trust

Incorrect uses of facial recognition can also affect public trust.

People may become less willing to trust organisations that use AI if they believe that automated systems can be used without sufficient safeguards. This is particularly important in policing because public cooperation and confidence are important to effective law enforcement.

Transparency about how facial recognition is used, how results are reviewed and what happens when errors occur can help organisations maintain accountability.

### 4.3 Discrimination and Unequal Consequences

If facial recognition systems perform differently across demographic groups, errors may not affect all communities equally.

This creates a wider social concern because repeated errors affecting particular groups could contribute to perceptions of unfair treatment or discrimination.

NIST's research supports the need to evaluate facial recognition performance across demographic groups rather than assuming that a system performs equally for everyone (Grother, Ngan and Hanaoka, 2019).

## 5. Positive Impacts

Facial recognition also has potential positive impacts when it is used appropriately.

It can help investigators search large collections of images and generate possible investigative leads. This may reduce the amount of time required to manually compare images.

In some contexts, facial recognition may also support identity verification, security and the identification of people who are wanted or missing.

However, these benefits depend on the accuracy of the technology and the procedures surrounding its use. A potential benefit does not justify treating a facial recognition result as conclusive evidence.

The positive impact of the technology can therefore be increased through appropriate testing, human oversight, clear procedures and independent verification.

## 6. Negative Impacts

The Robert Williams case demonstrates several potential negative impacts:

- False identification of an innocent person.
- Loss of freedom following an incorrect identification.
- Emotional and social consequences for the individual and their family.
- Potential unequal effects on different demographic groups.
- Reduced public confidence in AI and law enforcement.
- Lack of accountability if organisations rely too heavily on automated results.
- Legal and institutional consequences for organisations that use the technology improperly.

These impacts demonstrate why facial recognition should not be evaluated only by its technical capabilities. The consequences of errors and the context in which the technology is used are equally important.

## 7. Relationship Between Ethical, Legal and Social Impacts

The ethical, legal and social impacts are closely connected.

For example, an inaccurate facial recognition result is initially a technical problem. When people rely on that result without sufficient verification, it becomes an ethical issue involving accuracy and accountability. If the result contributes to an unjustified arrest, it can become a legal issue. The resulting loss of trust and harm to the individual and their family create wider social consequences.

The Robert Williams case therefore demonstrates how an error in an AI system can move across several areas of impact:

**Technical error → Human decision → Individual harm → Legal consequences → Social impact**

This relationship is important when evaluating AI systems because technical performance alone does not determine whether a system is responsible or appropriate.

## 8. Main Lessons from the Case

The case provides several important lessons for organisations using facial recognition.

First, facial recognition results should be treated as investigative leads rather than definitive evidence.

Second, organisations need to understand the limitations of the systems they use, including the possibility of false matches and demographic differences in performance.

Third, human oversight must involve meaningful independent verification rather than simply accepting an AI recommendation.

Finally, organisations need clear accountability mechanisms, including training, documentation, audits and appropriate legal safeguards.

## 9. Conclusion

The Robert Williams case demonstrates that facial recognition can provide useful investigative support while also creating significant ethical, legal and social risks.

The main ethical concerns include accuracy, fairness, human oversight and accountability. The legal consequences can involve individual rights and organisational responsibility, while the social consequences can include harm to individuals, families and public trust.

The case also demonstrates that these risks can be reduced through practical safeguards. Independent evidence, human oversight, staff training, system testing, auditing and clear policies can reduce the likelihood and consequences of incorrect identification.

Therefore, the responsible use of facial recognition should focus not only on improving the technology but also on ensuring that organisations use it carefully, transparently and with appropriate safeguards for the people affected by its decisions.
